# Children of the Sonoran Desert
## A Best Interests Child Advocate (BICA) Pilot

### Pilot Vision

The **Children of the Sonoran Desert Pilot** seeks to develop, implement, and evaluate a child-centered advocacy model for immigration-involved youth living within the Sonoran Desert region.

Grounded in principles of child welfare, trauma-informed care, educational advocacy, and interdisciplinary collaboration, the pilot will explore how a **Best Interests Child Advocate (BICA)** framework can strengthen support systems for vulnerable children navigating complex legal, educational, healthcare, and community environments.

> The Sonoran Desert is more than a geopolitical region. It is an interconnected ecological, cultural, and community landscape where children, families, institutions, and environments interact across generations. This initiative centers child well-being within that broader human ecosystem.

# Pilot Site Selection

## Recommended Initial Locations

- Arizona
- Southern California
- New Mexico
- Tribal and Indigenous communities within the Sonoran region

### Why Arizona?

Arizona offers a strong foundation for pilot implementation due to:

- Existing child welfare infrastructure
- Established CASA programs
- Higher education and research partnerships
- Community-based service networks
- Experience serving diverse child populations
- Opportunities for interdisciplinary collaboration

# Pilot Population

## Cohort A
### Unaccompanied Children

Children navigating immigration proceedings without a parent or legal guardian.

---

## Cohort B
### Accompanied Children

Children participating in immigration processes alongside parents, guardians, or sponsors.

---

## Cohort C
### Children Eligible for Specialized Relief

Including children seeking:

- Special Immigrant Juvenile Status (SIJS)
- Asylum protection
- Trafficking victim protections
- Refugee protections
- Humanitarian relief pathways

# Long-Term Vision

The **Children of the Sonoran Desert** initiative seeks to establish an evidence-informed model for child-centered advocacy that can be adapted across jurisdictions while remaining grounded in local communities, collaborative partnerships, and the well-being of children.

By integrating child welfare principles, educational advocacy, healthcare access, trauma-informed practice, and community engagement, this pilot aims to strengthen support systems for immigration-involved youth while advancing a broader vision of child-centered public policy.
