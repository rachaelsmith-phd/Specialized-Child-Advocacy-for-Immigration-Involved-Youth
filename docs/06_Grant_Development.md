# Best Interests Child Advocate (BICA) Initiative

## Working Grant Title

### *Building a Best Interests Child Advocate (BICA) Framework for Immigration-Involved Children: A Pilot Model for Federal, State, and Community Collaboration*

---

# Executive Summary

The **Best Interests Child Advocate (BICA) Initiative** proposes the development, implementation, and evaluation of a specialized child advocacy framework for immigration-involved youth.

Drawing upon established child advocacy models, particularly Court Appointed Special Advocate (CASA) and Guardian ad Litem (GAL) frameworks, the initiative seeks to strengthen child-centered decision-making across immigration, child welfare, educational, healthcare, and community systems.

The proposed model focuses on ensuring that children's developmental, educational, medical, psychological, familial, and long-term well-being considerations remain visible throughout the legal process.

---

# Funding Need

Children involved in immigration proceedings frequently interact with multiple legal, educational, healthcare, and social-service systems.

These systems perform important but distinct functions and often operate under separate legal mandates, organizational structures, and decision-making processes.

The BICA Initiative proposes the development of a structured advocacy model that:

- Facilitates communication across systems
- Documents child-centered concerns
- Supports coordinated decision-making
- Provides independent best-interests assessments
- Promotes consistent child-focused advocacy practices

---

# Problem Statement

Current systems provide:

- Immigration adjudication
- Legal representation
- Child placement services
- Family reunification services
- Educational supports
- Healthcare supports

Despite the availability of these services, responsibilities are frequently distributed across multiple agencies and jurisdictions.

As a result, no universally available advocate exists whose sole responsibility is to assess, document, and communicate the child's best interests throughout the entirety of the case process.

The proposed BICA model seeks to address this gap through structured advocacy, interdisciplinary collaboration, and evidence-informed decision support.

# Proposed Grant Objectives

## Objective 1
### Develop a National BICA Curriculum and Certification Program

Create a competency-based advocate training framework that integrates:

- Immigration systems knowledge
- Child development
- Trauma-informed practice
- Cultural responsiveness
- Documentation and reporting
- Interagency collaboration

---

## Objective 2
### Create Immigration Child Process Maps

Develop comprehensive process maps identifying:

- Child pathways through immigration systems
- Key decision-makers
- Child welfare considerations
- Existing protections
- Advocacy intervention points

---

## Objective 3
### Pilot BICA Implementation

Implement pilot programs in selected jurisdictions serving immigration-involved children.

Pilot sites may include:

- Immigration courts
- Community organizations
- Child welfare agencies
- Educational partnerships
- Legal service providers

---

## Objective 4
### Evaluate Child, Court, and System-Level Outcomes

Establish a structured evaluation framework measuring:

- Child outcomes
- Court outcomes
- Stakeholder experiences
- System performance indicators

---

## Objective 5
### Develop Replication and Scaling Recommendations

Produce evidence-based recommendations supporting future:

- Program expansion
- Federal-state partnerships
- Policy development
- Sustainable implementation models

# Five-Year Vision

The long-term vision of the BICA Initiative is the creation of a scalable, evidence-informed advocacy framework that can be adapted across jurisdictions while respecting existing federal, state, and local responsibilities.

The framework seeks to build upon existing child welfare and immigration systems rather than replace them by ensuring that children maintain a dedicated advocate focused on:

- Safety
- Permanency
- Well-being
- Development
- Education
- Healthcare
- Family relationships
- Long-term stability
