## A CASA-Informed Framework for Federal, State, and Community Collaboration

## Overview

Children involved in immigration proceedings occupy a unique and vulnerable position within the American legal system. Whether accompanied by parents or arriving as unaccompanied minors, children often navigate complex interactions among immigration enforcement agencies, immigration courts, child welfare systems, schools, healthcare providers, legal representatives, and community organizations.

While these systems are designed to fulfill distinct legal mandates, children frequently experience the effects of decisions made across multiple systems that may not be fully coordinated.

This project proposes a **Best Interests Child Advocate (BICA) Framework**, a child-centered advocacy model informed by the principles of Court Appointed Special Advocates (CASA) and adapted for immigration-related contexts.

---

# Problem Statement

Immigration proceedings primarily address:

- Legal status
- Admissibility
- Relief eligibility
- Family reunification
- Removal proceedings

Child welfare systems, by contrast, focus on:

- Child safety
- Permanency
- Well-being
- Family stability
- Best-interest determinations

These systems serve different but important purposes.

The challenge is not the failure of any single agency. Instead, children often move through systems designed around different statutory objectives, creating situations in which no single advocate is solely responsible for assessing and communicating the child's best interests throughout the entirety of the process.

As a result, developmental, educational, medical, psychological, and family-related considerations may receive less attention than legal outcomes.

---

# Existing Models and Opportunities

The United States already maintains several successful child advocacy models.

## Court Appointed Special Advocates (CASA)

CASA volunteers are trained to advocate for children involved in abuse and neglect proceedings by:

- Gathering information
- Monitoring case progress
- Communicating with courts
- Making recommendations
- Focusing on long-term child well-being

## Child Advocate Programs for Unaccompanied Children

Federal regulations authorize independent child advocates for certain vulnerable unaccompanied children. These advocates communicate with immigration courts and decision-makers and provide recommendations regarding the child's best interests.

## Community-Based Immigration Advocacy

Organizations such as the **Young Center for Immigrant Children's Rights** have demonstrated the value of specialized best-interest advocacy models for immigrant youth.

## Office of Refugee Resettlement (ORR)

The ORR administers programs supporting unaccompanied children and refugee youth through partnerships with:

- Service providers
- States
- Community organizations
- Educational institutions

These existing models demonstrate that many of the foundational components already exist.

What remains absent is a comprehensive, scalable framework that systematically applies child-centered advocacy principles across the broader population of immigration-involved children.

---

# Proposed Solution

## Best Interests Child Advocate (BICA) Framework

This project proposes the development of a specialized advocacy system designed to ensure that children maintain an independent voice focused exclusively on their long-term well-being throughout immigration-related proceedings.

The model would not replace:

- Attorneys
- Immigration judges
- DHS personnel
- ORR staff
- Social workers
- Parents or sponsors

Instead, advocates would complement existing roles by ensuring that child-centered considerations remain visible throughout decision-making processes.

---

## Core Advocacy Domains

The proposed advocate would assess and monitor:

### Child Safety

Protection from abuse, neglect, exploitation, trafficking, or unsafe placements.

### Family Relationships

Maintenance of healthy family connections and reunification opportunities when appropriate.

### Educational Continuity

Access to schooling, educational stability, and academic supports.

### Health and Mental Health

Identification of medical, developmental, psychological, and trauma-related needs.

### Placement Stability

Monitoring placement transitions and long-term permanency considerations.

### Developmental Well-Being

Evaluation of age-appropriate needs and developmental milestones.

### Cultural and Linguistic Factors

Recognition of language access, cultural identity, and community belonging.

### Long-Term Outcomes

Consideration of the child's future well-being beyond immediate case outcomes.

```text

           ↓

           ↓

           ↓
```

# Curriculum Development Framework

Drawing from CASA training principles and trauma-informed practice, the project would develop a specialized curriculum for Best Interests Child Advocates.

## Proposed Training Domains

### Immigration Systems and Processes

Federal agencies, immigration courts, and procedural pathways.

### Child and Adolescent Development

Developmental milestones and age-specific needs.

### Trauma, Migration, and Resilience

Impacts of displacement, family separation, and migration experiences.

### Cultural Responsiveness and Language Access

Strategies for effective communication across cultures and languages.

### Family Systems and Reunification

Understanding family structures and reunification processes.

### Best Interests Determinations

Evidence-informed assessment frameworks.

### Ethics and Professional Boundaries

Advocacy standards and professional conduct.

### Documentation and Court Reporting

Preparing reports and communicating recommendations.

### Interagency Collaboration

Navigating relationships among federal, state, and community stakeholders.

### Educational and Healthcare Advocacy

Supporting continuity of services across systems.

---

# Conclusion

Children involved in immigration proceedings often face some of the most complex legal and social circumstances in the United States. Existing systems perform critical and necessary functions, yet opportunities remain to strengthen child-centered advocacy across those systems.

Rather than creating an entirely new system, the **Best Interests Child Advocate (BICA) Framework** seeks to adapt proven child advocacy principles to an evolving policy environment. Through research, process mapping, curriculum development, and pilot implementation, this project aspires to strengthen collaboration among courts, agencies, communities, and families while improving outcomes for immigration-involved children.
